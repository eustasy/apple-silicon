The [MacBook Pro 14″](/devices/mac/macbook-pro-14/) replaces its base [M5](/chips/m5/base/10-10/) with the [12c CPU / 12c GPU M6](/chips/m6/base/12-12/), with pre-orders from 28 October and availability from 6 November. It is the first Mac laptop with the M6 and the first on Apple's 2nm process. The [M5 Pro](/chips/m5/pro/) and [M5 Max](/chips/m5/max/) configurations that [arrived in March](/news/march-2026-releases/) carry on unchanged.

With the [iMac moving to M6](/news/imac-m6/) the same day, the M6 is now in three Macs, alongside the [Mac mini](/devices/mac/mac-mini/).

|                                     | CPU                                    | GPU      | Neural Engine | Bandwidth        | Process |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------- | ---------------- | ------- |
| [M6 12c/12c](/chips/m6/base/12-12/) | 2 super + 4 performance + 6 efficiency | 12 cores | 2 × 16 cores  | 153 – 170.7 GB/s | 2nm     |
| [M5 10c/10c](/chips/m5/base/10-10/) | 4 super + 6 efficiency                 | 10 cores | 16 cores      | 153.6 GB/s       | 3nm     |

Apple's comparisons are against a 10c/10c M5 MacBook Pro with 32GB of memory: up to 30% faster graphics, up to 25% faster AI workloads on the GPU, up to 1.9x faster on the Neural Engine, and up to 1.3x faster Xcode builds and Blender renders.

### The cheapest MacBook Pro has the most Neural Engine cores

Because only the base chip moved on, the 14-inch range now spans two generations, with the newer one at the bottom.

|                                        | CPU                                    | GPU      | Neural Engine | Bandwidth       | Thunderbolt | Video playback |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------- | --------------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| [M6 12c/12c](/chips/m6/base/12-12/)    | 2 super + 4 performance + 6 efficiency | 12 cores | 2 × 16 cores  | Up to 170.7GB/s | 4           | 24 hours       |
| [M5 Pro 15c/16c](/chips/m5/pro/15-16/) | 5 super + 10 performance               | 16 cores | 16 cores      | 307 GB/s        | 5           | 22 hours       |
| [M5 Pro 18c/20c](/chips/m5/pro/18-20/) | 6 super + 12 performance               | 20 cores | 16 cores      | 307 GB/s        | 5           | 22 hours       |
| [M5 Max 18c/32c](/chips/m5/max/18-32/) | 6 super + 12 performance               | 32 cores | 16 cores      | 460 GB/s        | 5           | 20 hours       |
| [M5 Max 18c/40c](/chips/m5/max/18-40/) | 6 super + 12 performance               | 40 cores | 16 cores      | 614 GB/s        | 5           | 20 hours       |

**The $1,999 M6 has twice as many Neural Engine cores as the $4,099 M5 Max.** That is not the whole story for AI work, since the M5 Max has 40 GPU cores with Neural Accelerators against the M6's 12, and Apple has not published a TOPS figure for the M6. But it is the first time a MacBook Pro's base chip has had a bigger Neural Engine than the Pro and Max above it.

The M6 is also the only chip in the range with efficiency cores, as the M5 Pro and M5 Max use only super and performance cores. Apple rates it at up to 24 hours of video streaming, against 22 hours for the M5 Pro and 20 for the M5 Max in the same 14-inch body. That ties it with the 16-inch MacBook Pro with M5 Pro, in both its regular and [PROLED XDR](/news/macbook-pro-proled-xdr/) versions, for the longest of any Mac. The M6 does it from the 14-inch's 72.4-watt-hour battery, against 100 watt-hours in the PROLED 16-inch.

The M5 Pro and Max keep their advantages elsewhere: Thunderbolt 5 against the M6's Thunderbolt 4, three or four external displays against two, and far more memory bandwidth and capacity. The M6 tops out at 32GB, and starts at 1TB of storage.

### Apple confirms the 16GB bandwidth cap

Apple's buy page now lists the 16GB M6 at 153GB/s, and only the 24GB and 32GB configurations at 170GB/s. That is the split [recorded for the Mac mini in September](/news/mac-mini-mac-studio-refresh/), and it means **the base MacBook Pro 14″ gets no memory bandwidth increase**: a 16GB M6 runs at 153GB/s, against 153.6GB/s for the M5 it replaces.

The 10c/10c M5 leaves the MacBook Pro but stays on sale in the [MacBook Air](/devices/mac/macbook-air-13/) and [iPad Pro](/devices/ipad/pro/).
